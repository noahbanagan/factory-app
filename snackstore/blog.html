28 September 2026 · Snack history

Kinoko vs Takenoko: Japan’s 45-year snack war, explained

Meiji released Kinoko no Yama (mushroom mountain) in 1975 and Takenoko no Sato (bamboo-shoot village) in 1979. Same chocolate, different biscuit: Kinoko is a crisp cracker stem with a chocolate cap, Takenoko a soft cookie cone fully dipped. Japan has held national polls on it since 2001. Takenoko usually wins, and in our first two months Filipino customers agreed by about 60 to 40.

Our advice: buy one of each and hold your own vote.

10 September 2026 · Guide

How to read a Japanese snack label

Best-before. Look for 賞味期限 followed by a date, usually year.month.day in the Japanese order. 2027.03.15 means 15 March 2027. Reiwa-era dates (令和9年) also appear; add 2018 to get the Western year.

Allergens. Japan requires eight to be labelled: wheat (小麦), egg (卵), milk (乳), shrimp (えび), crab (かに), buckwheat (そば), peanut (落花生) and walnut (くるみ). Twenty more are recommended, including soy, pork and gelatin. We translate all of them on every product page.

Alcohol. 洋酒 or アルコール in the ingredient list means the product contains alcohol, common in some chocolates and premium KitKats.
